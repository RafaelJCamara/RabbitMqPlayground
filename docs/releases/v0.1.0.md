# RabbitMQ Playground v0.1.0: Build & route

> **Draft.** This is the text of the release, written on 2026-10-09 by S12. It becomes the body of the GitHub release when the two things in [ADR-0086](../adr/0086-the-release-waits-for-a-person-a-laptop-and-the-calendar-and-says-exactly-what-is-left.md) are true: the frame rate on a mid-range laptop, and seven scheduled nights of the Nightly in a row. Until then there is no tag, and the site that is deployed is the product as described here.

A browser-based, explanation-first RabbitMQ simulator for learning, teaching and designing topologies. You draw exchanges, queues, producers and consumers, link them on purpose, send messages and watch where each one goes, and the page tells you why. It runs entirely in the browser: no account, no server, nothing leaves your machine unless you share a link.

*Not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Broadcom Inc. or the RabbitMQ project.*

**Try it:** <https://rafaeljcamara.github.io/RabbitMqPlayground/>

## What is in it

- **Build.** A canvas with a toolbox, an inspector, rename, move, delete, undo and redo, auto-layout, zoom, light and dark themes, and shapes and badges that tell the kinds of node apart by more than colour. Exchanges are direct, fanout, topic and **headers**; queues are durable (as RabbitMQ 4.3 requires).
- **Link on purpose, five ways.** Drag from a dot, click the dot and then the target, the "Link to…" picker, the context menu, or the key `L` and the arrow keys. Every way ends in the same command, and a **command bar** (`/` or Ctrl/Cmd+K) does the same with a line of text, with completion, history, help and suggestions. A log of equivalent commands shows what each gesture was.
- **Route.** A deterministic simulation of how RabbitMQ 4.3 routes and delivers: the four exchange types, exchange-to-exchange bindings, cycles, at most one copy per queue, prefetch, manual and automatic acknowledgement, cancel against close, once, burst and interval producers, play, pause, step and speed. It is checked against the real broker by recorded fixtures and by a nightly run against RabbitMQ 4.3.
- **Explain.** An event log you can filter; a message inspector with the route in words; **Why?** and **why not?** lit on the canvas; a **what-if** tester that says where a message would go without sending it; a **topic tester** that shows which keys a binding key matches.
- **Headers exchanges, done properly.** `x-match` in four modes, typed values (`"1"` is not `1`), conditions for `exists`, the flags for names that are there twice and for `x-` names, a table of the headers of a message, a live table of recent messages against the conditions, and a binding made from a message by ticking its headers.
- **Several canvases.** A home with thumbnails, search and sort; tabs; rename, duplicate, delete and clear with a timed Undo; a backup of everything and its restore; JSON files that refuse a newer version; storage that asks the browser to keep it and warns when room runs out.
- **Share and export.** A link that holds the whole canvas in the address (and, if you choose, the messages in flight), opened in a view that keeps nothing unless you save a copy; a link that is too long to send is offered as a file. A `definitions.json` export for one virtual host, which lists what a broker cannot take. The Nightly imports the export into a real broker and replays routing.
- **Start fast.** A welcome that asks what to start with: six tutorials as templates (Hello World, Work Queues, Pub/Sub, Routing, Topics and Headers routing), a blank canvas, or a tour of six steps.
- **Accessible.** Everything can be done from the keyboard (one tab stop for the canvas, arrows to move between nodes, `L` to link, `M` to move, `P` to publish, `.` to step, `E` for the log); axe runs on every screen and state in both themes from a list that a test holds to the app; handles are 24 by 24 pixels; nothing that stays on screen covers the item that has the cursor. See [docs/accessibility.md](../accessibility.md).

## What is not in it

RPC, the other tutorials, connections and channels, durable and auto-delete lifecycles, `amq.*` exchanges, quorum queues and streams, plugins, a timeline you can rewind, and a back end. They are in the milestones after this one ([ADR-0003](../adr/0003-roadmap-and-milestones.md)).

## Compatibility

- The canvas file and the backup are **schema v1**. A file from a newer version is refused with the reason.
- A share link is **`v1.` followed by the deflated envelope in base64url**. Links made by this version will open in later versions; links made while the flags existed carried `?ff=…` in front of the fragment, and they still open.
- There are no feature flags left. The address and `localStorage` are not read for them.

## What was checked, and what was not

Checked by machines on every push: lint, format, types, about 6,100 tests of the libraries (engine and domain at 95% coverage or more, persistence at 85%), about 2,800 tests of the app, about 800 end-to-end tests in Chromium including axe in both themes, the bundle budgets, and the conformance fixtures; and every night against RabbitMQ 4.3 with the properties at 5,000 runs.

**Not checked by this release's author, and so not claimed:** a manual pass with NVDA or VoiceOver (a task for later, [#20](https://github.com/RafaelJCamara/RabbitMqPlayground/issues/20), that does not block this release: if you use a screen reader and something is wrong, please say so there); the frame rate on a mid-range laptop (the tool, `npm run perf:fps`, and the record of a gaming laptop are in [docs/performance.md](../performance.md)); and seven nights in a row. [ADR-0086](../adr/0086-the-release-waits-for-a-person-a-laptop-and-the-calendar-and-says-exactly-what-is-left.md) says how each is done.

## Known limits

- The editor is a diagram and does not reflow at 320 pixels wide (WCAG 1.4.10 allows it); the welcome and the home do.
- A shared link holds the whole canvas, readable by anyone who has it. There is no way to take a link back.
- A policy in a `meta` cannot stop the page from being framed (GitHub Pages sends no headers).
- A canvas that is saved keeps no simulation: it opens with the clock at 0.
